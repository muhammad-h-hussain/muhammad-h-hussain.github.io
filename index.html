About

I work in the Continuous Improvement team at the MHRA, the UK's Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency. My job is to help colleagues improve how work gets done: tightening processes, automating routine tasks, writing clear procedures and reporting on what is really happening.

I joined the MHRA in 2021 as a classifier granting medicines licences, at a time when the UK was rebuilding its regulatory processes after leaving the EU. That experience taught me how much careful coordination sits behind regulatory change, and how much good process design matters when deadlines are fixed in law.

Before the MHRA I worked in the NHS and the Ministry of Justice. I also founded and ran my own contracting company, providing gateline and security services to Govia (Southern/Thameslink), which gave me an early lesson in owning delivery end to end.

Project delivery

Department-wide SharePoint migration with zero reporting disruption

Context
My department's documents had to move from a retiring classic SharePoint site to a new modern site, as part of an agency-wide programme.
Challenge
175,491 files and folders across four document libraries. Several teams' Power BI reports and Power Automate flows read directly from files on the old site. A standard cutover would have broken live reporting on day one.
What I did
  • Acted as the single point of contact for the department.
  • Analysed the full export to find a review depth managers could actually use. Full depth gave 27,502 paths nested up to 28 levels. A first pass at three levels gave 220 paths but missed folders the export had mislabelled.
  • Fixed that by treating any path segment without a file extension as a folder. This gave 516 folder paths for managers to review.
  • Built the mapping template: fixed dropdown decisions, red/amber/green formatting, and blanks defaulting to "migrate like for like", so a missed reply could never cause data loss.
  • Flagged the reporting dependency two weeks before migration. Proposed a two-stage go-live: all content moved on schedule, and connected files switched over separately once their owners were ready.
  • Produced a 27 slide account of the migration for the head of department, including lessons for future waves.
Outcome
Completed 03/08/2026 with no interruption to live reporting. Seven days between the two go-lives. Access issues raised on go-live morning were resolved within two working days. After one agreed one-week extension, every date held. Formally signed off through change control.
Process and technology

Fixing error data that decides staff outcomes

Context
Individual error rates, measured against a 2% quality target, decide whether staff are placed on a formal quality review. The figures must be right.
Challenge
The underlying data was unreliable. The monthly reports managers received could not be reconciled with the rates used in management meetings.
What I did
  • Applied a Lean Six Sigma root cause approach.
  • Reviewed over 2,100 records and verified 112 individually against source.
  • Found a duplicate rate of around 6%, with 67 entries inflating counts. Error rates were overstated by up to 1.7 percentage points.
  • Traced five root causes: a manual copy step between sheets, free-text name entry, no date logic, no roster maintenance and no duplicate check.
  • Put a control on each: a fixed-list dropdown, a validation rule with a conditional formatting backstop, removal of the second sheet, automatic roster population and duplicate detection.
  • Rebuilt the monthly report as a dashboard on a validation-date basis, so it matches the management meeting figures. It shows KPI tiles, flagged staff, errors by team and category, and the top 10 work types. Two input cells drive it, so it can be reused every month.
Outcome
Decisions about staff now rest on verified figures. File size cut by 95%. The dashboard was presented to delivery managers in October 2026. I wrote the procedure that embeds the new controls.
Process and technology

Blue Prism robot performance analysis

Context
Some routine, rules based work is automated using Blue Prism robots (robotic process automation, or RPA).
Challenge
Without clear performance reporting, it was hard to see what the robots were really delivering.
What I did
  • Analysed three reporting periods, deduplicating 10,145 raw processing events to 5,200 unique submissions.
  • Set a baseline: 35.7% processed by the robot with no human input, 42.0% out of scope, and 22.2% failed or flagged.
  • Categorised 4,040 error events. Out of scope was the largest category.
  • Identified that post-upgrade figures could not be trusted. A technical fault stopped failures being recorded, so an apparent 63.2% success rate was overstated.
  • Built a six-sheet, auditable Excel report with live formulas.
Outcome
Gave decision-makers an accurate picture of what the automation was delivering, which informed a decision on the future of the automation programme. Recognised with a manager-nominated award for testing, troubleshooting and supplier coordination during the automation platform upgrade.
